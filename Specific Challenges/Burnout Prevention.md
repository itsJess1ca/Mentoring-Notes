# Common causes of Burnout
Burnout often stems from chronic workplace stress with very little gratification. I see it most when a team is just constantly pushing the next thing, and not taking a break after a release to appreciate the win. Other workplace stressors include:
 - Excessive workload
 - Poor work-life balance
 - Insufficient Social Support
 - Lack of control over your tasks
Perfectionism, low self-esteem, and "imposter syndrome" often tend to exaggerate this.

# Stages of burnout
In my experience, burnout tends to be somewhat cyclical and comes from a constant desire to prove yourself.
## Proving yourself
You've just had a moment of clarity and things start falling into place. You're getting tickets done quicker than normal, you're on the ball and things seem easy. You start taking on more tickets, and more one-off tasks from other members of your team.
### Notes
- For me, this tends to happen when:
	- Starting a new job
	- Getting a promotion
	- After a performance review (good or bad, I just feel the need to improve constantly)
## Fatigue
Those extra tasks are maybe starting to take longer, or are feeling a bit more overwhelming. You're struggling to keep up and definitely aren't as motivated as you were before.
### Notes
- If you notice you're at this point, stop taking on those extra tasks *(They should be going through your PM's anyway for prioritisation)*
- Make a list of everything you've got to get done, and work on it one at a time. Nothing is more of a priority than your mental health.
	- If you create that list and realise there's things others could be doing or that you're not going to get it done in a timely manner, send a message to your lead/pm to potentially redistribute these tasks.
## Exhaustion
You don't want to get here... You're getting barely any work done, and perhaps people are noticing (or at least it feels like they are). At this point, it's important to reach out and mention you're overwhelmed and burnt out. Take a few days off, or just take on less tasks, perhaps you can get some help from someone else on your team via pairing. 
### Notes
- At this phase I often feel terrible, and imposter syndrome sets in quite hard. It's not fun.
	- It's important to know that you got here through doing too much, you've KILLED IT recently, but now you're suffering for it.


# Communicate
If you start feeling like you're getting overwhelmed easier, or aren't quite as "on the ball" as normal, communicate it early. Everyone in this industry knows burnout, we've all been through it. Support for burnout is getting significantly better recently and companies are doing a lot more to support people in this. If you communicate this early, then a temporary reduction of responsibilities, support getting things wrapped up, or even just a "take the day, no worries" can come from it and these do really help.

# Use your holidays!
So many times in my career I've been "on a roll" and not wanted to take holiday, or thought "I should save it for when I *really* need it" - this is something I've been gradually getting better at.
My manager at Hometree always tells me "There's a reason kids get so many holidays from school", and he's absolutely right. Just taking a couple extra days off every 6-8 weeks helps have that "reset", or something to always look forward to.

# Have study leave? Use it!
When I worked at Beamery we got "dev days", the first Thursday of every month where we had freedom to work on anything "off sprint". Whether that was reading, a personal project, or something that could make your work easier within the job. 
This was a fixed thing that the entire tech department took, no questions.

However, at Hometree we get 12 days of "study leave" a year. I never used to use this myself, as I thought "I'm not studying for anything, I'll save it until I am". I've started treating this the same as Beamerys dev days though, and it's been a huge help. I now know that those little things that annoy me, or that little task I've been wanting to do. I always have a day coming up that I can allocate to it. It's a nice little way to keep things ticking.

# Recovery Techniques

When you're already feeling burnt out, it's about getting back to a sustainable rhythm rather than just "pushing through". I've found these approaches actually work:

## Immediate Recovery (Days/Weeks)
- **Take proper time off** - Not "I'll just check emails" days, but actual disconnected time off. Your brain needs the break.
- **Set strict boundaries** - Things like setting a proper lunch break in your calendar and actually taking it. I also automate my Slack status to show "out of office" after 6pm so people know not to expect immediate responses.
- **Drop the non-essentials** - That side project at work? The "quick favor" for another team? Let them go for now. Your main responsibilities are enough.

## Medium-term Reset (Weeks/Months)
- **Regular check-ins with your manager** - I schedule these every 2 weeks when I'm recovering, just to make sure we're aligned on priorities and I'm not taking on too much again.
- **Build buffer time** - I started adding 25% extra time to my estimates. Sounds like padding, but it's actually just realistic planning when you're not at 100%.
- **Pair more often** - When you're burnt out, everything feels harder. Pairing sessions help share the mental load and you'll often find the work goes faster anyway.

## Long-term Prevention
- **Sustainable work rhythms** - I block 30 minutes at the start of each day for planning, and 30 minutes at the end for wrapping up. No meetings, no slack. Just thinking time.
- **Regular skill building** - Counterintuitively, learning new things actually helps prevent burnout. When you feel more capable, work feels less overwhelming.
- **Build your support network** - Both inside work (people you can pair with, bounce ideas off) and outside work. Sometimes you just need to vent to someone who gets it.

# Having Difficult Conversations

These conversations are awkward, but they're way less awkward than burning out completely and having to explain why your performance dropped off a cliff.

## With Your Manager
**The key is being proactive rather than reactive.**

*"I've been reflecting on my workload lately and I'm concerned I might be taking on too much. Can we chat about priorities and maybe redistribute a few things? I want to make sure I'm delivering quality work rather than just trying to do everything."*

**If you're already struggling:**

*"I need to be honest - I've been feeling overwhelmed and I think it's starting to impact my work. I'd like to discuss how we can get me back to a sustainable workload. I've made a list of everything on my plate if that would be helpful to review together."*

## With Teammates
**When someone asks you to take on extra work:**

*"I'm at capacity right now with \[specific examples]. Could we revisit this next sprint, or is there someone else who might be available?"*

**When you need to delegate or ask for help:**

*"I've got \[specific task] on my plate but I'm struggling to give it the attention it deserves. Would you be able to take a look, or could we pair on it? I think it would actually go faster with two people."*

## With Yourself
This might sound silly, but sometimes the hardest conversation is the one in your own head.

*"I'm doing good work, I just need to slow down a bit. Taking a break isn't lazy, it's maintenance."*

*"That thing I didn't finish today? It'll still be there tomorrow. The world won't end if I leave the office at 5:30."*
