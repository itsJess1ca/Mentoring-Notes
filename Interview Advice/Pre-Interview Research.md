## Company Research Strategies and Tools

### Essential Research Areas
- **Company mission and values** - Not just what's on their website, but what they actually seem to prioritize
- **Recent news and growth** - Funding rounds, new products, leadership changes, market position
- **Team structure and culture** - How they work, what they value, who you'd be working with

### Useful Research Tools and Platforms
- **LinkedIn** - Check out team members, company updates, and employee backgrounds
- **Glassdoor** - Read reviews but take them with a grain of salt. Look for patterns, not individual complaints
- **Company blog/engineering blog** - Shows their technical approach and culture better than marketing pages

### Red Flags to Watch For During Research
- High turnover in the role you're applying for (check LinkedIn for previous people in similar positions)
- Consistent complaints about management or work-life balance in reviews
- Job posting that's been up for months - might indicate unrealistic expectations or internal issues

## Understanding Team Structure and Challenges

### Questions to Research About the Team
- Who would be your direct manager? Look them up - how long have they been there, what's their background?
- What's the team size and experience level? Are you joining a senior team or would you be the most experienced?
- How long have team members been at the company? Lots of recent hires might mean growth or turnover

### Identifying Team Dynamics and Culture
- Check if team members are active on social media about their work - usually indicates good culture
- Look for signs of knowledge sharing - do they have tech talks, blog posts, open source contributions?
- Notice how they talk about their work in job posts - exciting challenges vs. just listing technologies

### Understanding the Role's Context Within the Organization
- Is this a new role or a replacement? New roles often mean growth and opportunity for scope expansion
- Where does this team sit in the company structure? Close to revenue usually means more resources and attention
- What problems are they trying to solve? Understanding the 'why' helps you ask better questions

## Preparing Thoughtful Questions About the Role

### Questions About Day-to-Day Responsibilities
- "What does a typical week look like for someone in this role?"
- "How much of the time is spent on new feature development vs. maintenance?"
- "What tools and processes does the team use for development and deployment?"

### Questions About Growth and Development
- "What opportunities are there for learning and skill development?"
- "How do you support career progression for people in this role?"
- "What would success look like in this position after 6 months? After a year?"

### Questions About Company Culture and Values
- "How would you describe the company culture?" (But listen for specifics, not buzzwords)
- "What do you enjoy most about working here?"
- "How does the company handle work-life balance?" (Especially important if you're coming from a redundancy situation)

### Questions About Team Dynamics and Collaboration
- "How does the team collaborate on projects?"
- "What's the process for making technical decisions?"
- "How do you handle disagreements or conflicts within the team?"
