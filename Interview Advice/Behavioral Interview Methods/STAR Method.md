## What is STAR?

[STAR](https://uk.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/star-technique) is your friend. Try and have some examples prepared for common questions. Consult your [[Brag Doc]] if you have one to help with these.

**S**ituation - Set the scene and context
**T**ask - What was your responsibility or goal?
**A**ction - What specific actions did you take?
**R**esult - What was the outcome?

## When to Use STAR

### Best Suited Scenarios
- Questions about specific projects or achievements
- When you need to demonstrate your role within a team effort
- Stories where context and background are crucial to understanding your contribution

### Question Types That Work Well with STAR
- "Tell me about a time when..."
- "Give me an example of when you had to..."
- "Describe a situation where you..."

## Breaking Down Each Component

### Situation: Setting the Scene
- Keep it brief but provide enough context
- Set the stage without getting lost in unnecessary details
- Focus on relevant background that helps the interviewer understand why this mattered

### Task: Defining Your Role
- Be specific about what YOU were responsible for
- Don't just describe what the team did - focus on your piece
- Make it clear why this task was important or challenging

### Action: Your Specific Contributions
- This is the meaty part - spend most of your time here
- Use "I" statements, not "we" - even in team settings, focus on your actions
- Be specific about the steps you took and decisions you made

### Result: Measurable Outcomes
- Quantify when possible ("reduced load time by 40%", "saved 2 hours per week")
- Include both immediate and long-term impacts
- Don't forget to mention what you learned or how it influenced future work

## Common STAR Mistakes to Avoid

### Being Too Vague or Too Detailed
- Avoid generic statements like "I worked hard" or "the team was successful"
- Don't get bogged down in technical minutiae that doesn't serve the story
- Find the sweet spot where you're specific enough to be credible but concise enough to keep attention

### Focusing on Team vs. Individual Actions
- It's tempting to be modest and say "we" did everything
- Interviewers want to know what YOU specifically contributed
- You can acknowledge team effort while still highlighting your individual role

### Weak or Unmeasurable Results
- "Everyone was happy" isn't a result - be more specific
- Try to include numbers, percentages, or other concrete measures
- If you can't quantify, describe the qualitative impact clearly

## Practice Examples

### Leadership Example
**Situation:** During a critical project deadline, our lead developer had to take emergency leave
**Task:** As a mid-level dev, I needed to coordinate the remaining team and ensure we hit our launch date
**Action:** I organized daily standups, redistributed tasks based on everyone's strengths, and took on the most complex features myself
**Result:** We launched on time, the client was thrilled, and my manager recognized my leadership potential in my next review

### Problem-Solving Example
**Situation:** Users were reporting our mobile app was crashing on older Android devices
**Task:** I was assigned to investigate and fix the issue with limited reproduction ability
**Action:** I set up a testing environment with older devices, identified the memory leak in our image loading, and implemented a more efficient caching solution
**Result:** Crash rates dropped by 85% and user retention improved by 12% over the following month

### Teamwork Example
**Situation:** Our design and development teams were constantly clashing over feature specifications
**Task:** As the developer liaison, I needed to improve collaboration and reduce back-and-forth
**Action:** I initiated weekly design-dev sync meetings, created a shared specification template, and established a review process
**Result:** Feature delivery time decreased by 30% and both teams reported much better working relationships in our quarterly survey
