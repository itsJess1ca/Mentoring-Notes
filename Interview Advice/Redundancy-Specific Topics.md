## Explaining the Redundancy

### How to Frame Redundancy Positively
- "The company went through a restructuring and my role was made redundant" - Simple, factual, no emotion
- Focus on the business decision rather than personal impact: "Due to budget constraints, they eliminated several positions including mine"
- Don't over-explain or sound defensive - confidence is key

### Preparing a Confident 2-3 Sentence Explanation
- Practice this until it feels natural: "My previous company went through restructuring and my position was eliminated. It's given me the opportunity to find a role that better aligns with my career goals, which is why I'm excited about this position."
- Keep it brief - don't turn it into a story about company politics or unfairness
- Always pivot to why you're interested in THEIR role

### Addressing Potential Employer Concerns About Being "Let Go"
- Be clear it wasn't performance-related: "It was purely a business decision - several roles across different departments were affected"
- If possible, mention if you were asked to stay longer or help with transition: "They actually asked me to stay an extra month to help train my replacement"
- Offer references from your previous manager if they're supportive

## Emotional Preparation

### Managing Interview Anxiety During Job Loss Stress
- Remember: you're interviewing them too. This isn't just about them choosing you
- Practice your redundancy explanation with friends until it doesn't trigger emotional responses
- Schedule interviews for times when you feel most confident (for me, that's usually just after lunch)

### Building Confidence After Redundancy
- Review your [[Brag Doc]] before every interview - remind yourself of your achievements
- Redundancy often happens to good people in tough business situations. It's not a reflection of your worth
- Use this time to be selective - you don't have to take the first offer that comes along

### Dealing with Rejection While Already Vulnerable
- Interview rejection after redundancy can feel like a double blow, but it's rarely about you personally
- Set a limit - if you get emotional after a rejection, take the rest of the day off from job searching
- Remember that every "no" gets you closer to the right "yes" - and right now, fit is more important than ever
